# L3 DB12: entry readers on the byte pool (partial)

Brief: entry Pruv readers and writers on the WTF-8 byte pool, drop the whole-packet `wire_in` / `wire_out` expansion,
outputs byte-identical, cells at or below DB10 (time M 0.927x, L 0.849x; RSS M 0.766x, L 0.693x).

## What landed

- `bench/l3/entry/root.pruv` `index_step`: the adapter passes the emitted await back as both `prev` and `emitted`.
  When the two wire arrays have the same length and words, `wire_in` runs once and the result is passed as both
  arguments. `wire_in` is a pure function of its words, so the packets are the same as before. Different words still
  decode separately and `entry_api::index_step` compares them as before. A normal op 9 step now expands 3 packets
  instead of 4.

No `ent_*.pruv` file changed, so the decoder, the validation and the slot-bounds logic are the DB10 code.

## Why the byte-pool readers did not land

A research sweep (Codex gpt-6.2-astra) of the pool sites found that the UTF-16 unit is part of the observable layout,
not only of the storage:

- Slots are `[6, unit_off, unit_len]`. The entry host (`bench/l3/entry/host.rs`) and the req host (`bench/l3/req/leaf.rs`)
  apply those offsets to a unit array, and req calls `entry_api` directly with unit packets.
- Op 9 keeps expanded-packet word addresses in its await (table start, pool high-water, expanded length) and in
  `progress`. A byte pool changes those numbers and the compact/full and `InputTooLarge` choices that use them.
- Truncation at 200 units (`ent_dispatch`) and 80 units (`req_fill`) can end on a high surrogate, and neighbouring
  slots can share one 4-byte form in the pool. A per-slot byte range is not a slot's text.
- `req_list` orders keys by UTF-16 code units (sort and binary search).

So moving the readers to bytes needs a slot coordinate change shared by both hosts, the op 9 state layout and req.
That does not fit the 2 h cap with proofs. It stays as D-DB12-1.

D-DB10-6 / D-DB8-4 were not done: the `exact_limit` probe still pads section 0 with zero words, and moving the
padding changes the probe's packet expectation (outside this lane).

## Perf

`perf_r02.py` (7 runs per cell), 15 alternating rounds of the DB10 harness (base) and the DB12 harness (new) on one
machine, 20:11 to 20:13 KST. Load average was 15.9 at the start, 16 to 29 during the rounds and 24.3 at the end.
Value = median over the 15 rounds of Pruv / hand.

| cell | DB10 limit | base (this run) | new (this run) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| time M | 0.927x | 0.988x | 0.931x |
| time L | 0.849x | 0.866x | 0.850x |
| RSS M | 0.766x | 0.749x | 0.722x |
| RSS L | 0.693x | 0.649x | 0.646x |

Both RSS cells are under the DB10 limits. Under this load the time cells are noisy: single rounds range from 0.57x to
1.45x, and the unchanged DB10 base also measures above its own DB10 limits (0.988x, 0.866x; DB10 measured at load 6
to 9). New is at or below base in every cell, so no cell got worse in the same-load comparison. The time cells sit
0.004x and 0.001x above the DB10 numbers, which is inside the noise. The change only removes one `wire_in` call.

req `perf_req.py --no-node --sizes 1000,5000` ran once with rc 0. Growth exponents from S 1000 to 5000 are 0.98 to
1.17 (none suspect-quadratic). The spec 13 call-count assertion holds for every finished run.
